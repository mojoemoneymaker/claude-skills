# Delivery

How a line is said. This file feeds the delivery notes block that follows the spoken text; it
never goes inside the spoken block. Attribution: Joe's own notes from Eli Wilde's live event,
2024-10-04 (Speaking Fundamentals), and 2024-10-05, unless marked otherwise. These are Joe's
paraphrase, not the teacher's words.

## Before speaking: the state

All decisions are state decisions, and the speaker cannot control the audience's state without
controlling his own. Two keys: focus and breath. Before stepping on, or before pressing record,
embody four things:

1. **Belief.** A feeling of certainty about what is being said and about the listener.
   Believe this is the thing.
2. **Physiology.** Energy up, body organised (see Presence).
3. **Outcome.** What the listener should feel, believe and think at the end. Decide it first.
4. **Rapport.** Trust and responsiveness. Two openers the notes record: acknowledging what the
   listener has already done ("you've already built this far, now..."), and the permission ask
   before something personal ("Can I share something personal with you?").

Closer composure: centred and calm. Conviction corrects tonality; a line the speaker believes
comes out right.

## Focus

Two kinds: peripheral (relaxed, takes in the room) and foveal (locked on one point). Speak from
peripheral, land lines in foveal, on one person.

## Breath

Get comfortable pausing. Commas are a breath, full stops are a swallow. The exercise from the
notes: read a book out loud and pay attention only to vision and breath. For the teleprompter,
this is why the read-aloud pass caps a sentence at one breath.

## Presence

Stand with feet straight and not too wide, hips in line, chest slightly lifted, take up space.
The stressed body gives itself away: feet wide and turned out with hips pushed forward and
weight back, hands in pockets, or all the weight on one side. On camera the same rules apply
from the waist up.

## Vocal modes

Three modes, and the heartbeat of a message is the movement between them. Locked into one
mode, any speaker goes flat.

| Mode | Sound | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Sage** | Medium, even, unhurried. The guide, the wise one. Hypnotic language. | Setup, framing, the reframe itself, answering questions |
| **Warrior** | Short, punchy, raised energy. | The turn, the call to do something, the rehook |
| **Lover** | Slow, long, longer pauses. Kinesthetic, so the listener feels it. Requires confidence in staying still. | The close, the personal story, the line that matters most |

**The charisma pattern:** Sage first, Warrior second, Lover third. Open as the guide, lift into
the call, land in stillness. For a reel that is: setup in Sage, the belief turn in Warrior, the
CTA in Lover. For an MC grand entrance: the build in Sage, the names in Warrior, the couple's
entrance line in Lover then Warrior for the room's cue.

Exercise from the notes: count numbers aloud in each mode until the modes are distinct.

## Gestures

- **Warrior:** one finger up, like a sword, never pointed at the listener. It appears and then
  it is gone. The soft form retracts the finger into a closed hand, as if holding the secret.
  No waggle.
- **Lover:** open hands, below the waist. Nothing up the sleeve. Communicates trust.
- **Sage:** the thinking pause, hand still, used while appearing to consider, which draws the
  listener in. Also the right gesture while taking a question.
- **Sovereign:** an L drawn across with the hand, naturally down and across, for the
  authoritative statement.

## Eyes

- Keep the peripheral view; do not tunnel.
- Neither sprinkler eyes (sweeping) nor laser eyes (locked). Pause, then look.
- Never speak to many. Always speak to one, chosen at random, then another.
- Smile with the eyes. Every line is a gift to the person receiving it.
- On camera: the lens is the one person. Hold it through the pause.

## Face

A slight smile most of the time. The feeling behind it, from the notes: "I know something you
don't know", "I care about you", "I want to have fun with you". An animated face reads; a still
face reads as absent.

## Rhythm

Statement, then the question a listener would ask about it, then the answer. That is the
cadence of a speaker who is easy to listen to, and it is the one thing in this file that comes
from a verbatim source: "I said a statement and then I immediately asked myself a question
about the statement I just made. And that sets up my next statement." (Eli Wilde, speaker
drills video, 10:39) Four statements in a row go flat. A self-question resets the ear.

Three spoken devices that carry the rhythm, all from the same session:

- The self-question. "So how do we actually do this? Well..."
- Call and response. "What's it called? Question control." The room says the word.
- The attention generator before the point. "Here's the part most people miss."

Joe's rhythm will not sound like Eli's. "It's a rhythm. And how you do the same thing, it'll
sound different from you." (15:19) Write the self-questions in Joe's words, at Joe's pace.

## Writing delivery notes

Keep them short, and only where they change how a line is said. The shape:

- L3: pause before "it isn't". Warrior on "it isn't", drop back to Sage.
- Last line: Lover. Slow it down, hold the lens through the full stop.
- Grand entrance names: Warrior, one beat between each pair, open hand toward the doors.

Never write a delivery note the speaker would have done anyway. The notes are for the two or
three places the reading could go wrong.
