Lesson: Question control (interrogate your own content)
Rule: Make a statement, then immediately ask yourself the question a listener would ask about it, then answer it. The answer is the next statement. Repeat.
Why: "So it's a statement, and then immediately ask yourself a question." (10:05) "I said a statement and then I immediately asked myself a question about the statement I just made. And that sets up my next statement." (10:39) "So I just did four statements. It started to get boring, yeah?" (10:33) "if I start asking questions around it ... it's more believable ... it overwhelms their mind in a way where they have less uncertainty if I interrogate my own content." (13:24) "it just breaks up the monotony." (15:22)
Example: "Stagecraft is how you capture and maintain attention. So many people out there speak and speak and speak and speak, and they might even have good things they say, but it doesn't convert because they don't keep, what's the word? Attention." (11:02) The drill: "Write a sentence like my favorite color is blue. What's a question I can ask before? What's a question I can ask after?" (12:49) Answer to a participant asking whether the question is the one the audience is thinking: "Yeah, I've thought about that I think they're thinking, too. But sometimes ... I'm just kind of making it up on the spot, and it makes it more flowy." (15:41)
Scopes: teaching, talks, reels, sales calls (the self-question replaces a monologue)
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: Call and response ("What's it called? Question control.")
Rule: Ask the room to say the key word back. "What's it called? Question control." (11:14) "What kind of speaking? Powerful speaking." (12:38) "What is it? Say it again. Action." (14:31)
Why: The key word said by the room is the key word remembered by the room. It is repetition and intensity done by the listener.
Scopes: teaching, workshops, mentorship calls. In a reel, the viewer answers in their head, so the question is asked and the word is said once, plainly.
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: Attention generators
Rule: Spoken signposts that reset attention before a point. Verbatim list from the session (18:17): "Here's what you need to remember. Bottom line is this. Here's the most important thing you want to think about. Here's why it's so important. Here's the crazy part. Check this out. Here's the fun part." Also "this might shock you" (18:30) and "Now here's what you need to realize." (18:52)
Why: "80% of influence is focus, it's controlling focus, and people's focus begins to wane." (18:38) "the two questions in your audience's mind is, one, can I trust this person, and two, can I listen to this person." (20:05) "And it's not your fault. It's the speaker's fault." (20:24)
Example: "So what's the number one most common thing holding people back? Well, this might shock you. Now I just increased your dopamine." (18:30)
Scopes: all; in a reel these are the rehooks (the wdjm-script-writer rehook rule already carries Joe's versions)
Conflict: none, with one note: "this might shock you" is a curiosity opener and only earns its place when what follows would in fact surprise the listener
Status: adopted

Lesson: The thesis pattern (one word, then why, then what it means)
Rule: Provocative claim that lands on one word. Self-question why. Answer. Self-question what that means. Answer with the implication.
Why: it is question control applied to a whole point, and it gives the listener the word to hold.
Example: "By far, the number one most common thing holding people back from getting the life they want comes down to one word, action. So why is action the number one thing holding people back? We are living in a world full of people who have all the goals in the world, but they never take action." (14:00) Then: "So what does it mean to take action? It means if you want to actually see drastic results in your life, you need to be ready to take a little risk and actually go for something that you want." (15:07) The tennis version: "the number one most common thing holding all tennis players back from getting to the level they want comes down to one word. Confidence. Yes. So why is confidence number one thing?" (16:33)
Scopes: teaching openers, reels, talks
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: It's a rhythm, and yours will sound different
Rule: "It's a rhythm. And how you do the same thing, it'll sound different from you. And you'll ask yourself questions, and it just breaks up the monotony." (15:19)
Why: The pattern is the skeleton; the voice is the speaker's. Copying the phrasing sounds copied.
Example: The self-quote device: "Somebody once told me that if I want to be successful, I need to start speaking. And I thought, is that true?" (15:52) followed by a chain of questions to the room ending in "maybe it means we need to get better at this one thing called what? Speaking." (16:26)
Scopes: all
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: Two sales, and the process comes before the product
Rule: "There's two sales the audience needs to be sold on." (04:13) First the approach: "They need to believe the approach, vehicle, process to achieve." (04:29) Then: "you believe your product is the best way for them to take advantage of the approach and vehicle." (04:40) "it's hard to make the second sale for your product unless you've made the first." (09:13)
Why: The crypto example (03:49): a trading software pitched to someone not yet sold on crypto. "What if the person isn't sold on crypto? So this is selling the product before we got into the process."
Example: The dance-lessons client: "the key to your great relationship, this great life, is having these immersive experiences. And the best way to improve this bonding with your wife is through dance. See how I'm setting it up so all of the content I'm anchoring to that." (06:54) Eli's own: "I want the audience to believe that the life they desire is on the other side of their communication skills." (04:56)
Scopes: sales calls, talks, reels that carry a CTA. For a couple the first sale is "the reception is run by one voice, and who that voice is decides the night"; the DJ is the second.
Conflict: none in the mechanism
Status: adopted (this is the Trust Trinity's third leg, now with Eli's own words)

Lesson: The through line
Rule: One thesis, said over and over, that every piece of content anchors to. "The quality of your life is the quality of communication. And you cannot not communicate." (05:02) "So over and over, I say things like, the quality of your life is the quality of communication." (05:02) "I just keep on saying things like that. That's all ... anchors to my through line." (05:38) Step one of the written drill: "write out what is your vehicle or process." (07:07)
Why: Repetition installs the first sale before the second is ever mentioned.
Scopes: talks, a content series, a mentorship curriculum; Joe's DJ MOJOE version is already approved copy ("A night your guests keep talking about, and one you actually get to enjoy") and the WDJM Core 6 beliefs are the candidates for the WDJM through line
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: Demonstrate the gap, do not describe it
Rule: "I pull people on stage and I speak better than them most of the time. So they see there's a gap and they like it and there's a demonstration and they're like, wow." (05:07)
Why: A shown skill is believed; a claimed skill is doubted.
Scopes: talks, workshops; for Joe's reels the demonstration is the footage (the a cappella end of night, the hip-hop to Spanish transition), not a claim about it
Conflict: none, adapted so the demonstration never humiliates a participant
Status: adapted

Lesson: Reframes must stay connected, or they are recitation
Rule: The basic shape, verbatim: "It's not this. It's that. And here's what it means. That is the most basic reframe." (02:45) The warning: a rep who "said money aside like eight times ... there's no connection. It just, she completely lost the frame." (02:04) "It's so amateur when people say money aside, money aside." (02:04)
Why: "Why you need it: to create an environment inside the audience's mind or the individual's mind that it's more likely to positively change and gives them a fresh look or concept without past restrictions." (02:30)
Example: "You're not scared. You're just excited because you're stepping into the unknown. And that's a good thing, isn't it?" (03:00) "Pressure is a privilege. A privilege of having something meaningful to strive for." (03:00)
Scopes: all
Conflict: none for the mechanism
Status: adopted (extends the reframe lesson from Joe's notes; the "money aside" warning is now the anti-pattern's source)

Lesson: A frame is control of focus, and two levers run every belief
Rule: "the frame is like controlling somebody's focus." (23:27) "to be a builder and breaker of belief and a master and molder of meaning." (24:59) "whatever you focus on, you feel, two levers run every belief, focus and meaning. So I'm shifting focus and I'm changing the meaning." (25:16)
Why: The window image (23:21): look out the front window and you see one thing, out the back door another. The speaker decides which window.
Example: The results chain (25:54): "results in people's lives come from their actions, actions come from decisions, decisions come from our thoughts and feelings, and all of our thoughts and feelings come from our beliefs ... all of your beliefs have come from your experiences or references." Then the question: "is it possible that some of the beliefs that you've created from references in your past could be disempowering you in some way? Is that possible?" (26:18)
Scopes: teaching, talks, mentorship
Conflict: the results chain and the "is it possible" question are honest teaching. The line "it's not that you don't have money, it's just that you don't have the skills" (23:48) is a redefine that, said to a couple, plants a deficiency and is refused for that scope; said to a DJ who asked why bookings are flat it is Joe's own teaching and is adapted
Status: adopted (mechanism), the money redefine adapted and restricted to the mentorship room

Lesson: The story arc: conflict, loss, then delay, deny, distract
Rule: "all stories follow an arc ... there's a conflict and then there's a sense of loss. And then when people experience a sense of loss, they usually do one of three things. They delay, they deny, or they get distracted." (22:10) "Most of you are only operating in the first half." (22:10)
Why: The listener recognises their own delay, denial or distraction in the story before the speaker names it. Lion King is the worked example (22:29): Simba, the loss, Hakuna Matata as the distraction.
Example: "What if your biggest pain could be your biggest source of impact in the world because your story has the power to shift people's worlds." (21:38)
Scopes: Joe's own arc in talks and reels (the before, mistake, realisation, after of wdjm-script-writer's Story mode maps onto this)
Conflict: "Most of you have a victim story, a complaining story" (21:57) said to a room is a label; not used on couples, and in the mentorship room only as an invitation, never a verdict
Status: adopted for structure, the labelling refused

Lesson: Surface, source, self
Rule: "surface, source, and self, I walk them down it." (27:26) The worked chain: why are you here, I want to make more money; why do you think that is, I don't know how; what would it mean to you, I'd be a good father. (27:39) "deepest frame is identity, you're not selling a new way of thinking, you're selling a different version of themselves." (30:35)
Why: The self layer is where the decision lives.
Scopes: sales calls, mentorship
Conflict: same as three layers of pain: used to aim and to understand, never to press
Status: adapted (merged with three layers of pain; this is the same ladder with Eli's names for the rungs)

Lesson: Wrap the question: acknowledge before you move on
Rule: Answer what they said before asking the next thing. "I totally get that, and the fact that you're wanting to improve this already puts you ahead of so many people." (28:08) "I recontextualize the question before his answer with my feedback. I didn't just go to the next question. How long have you had this problem? A year? Okay. And how's that been impacted on you? ... it jumps ahead too much. It's the wrapping of the question." (28:29)
Why: The listener is rewarded for saying something true and goes further next time.
Example: "Yeah, I get it. Sometimes we're not really clear. Totally cool." (28:52)
Scopes: sales calls, mentorship calls, any interview
Conflict: the second half of this lesson, "I can take any bad answer and make it into a good answer because I know what a good answer sounds like" (29:48), puts words in the listener's mouth. Refused for couples; for DJs, reflect what they said and name what is useful in it, never rewrite it
Status: adapted

Lesson: The version-of-you sequence (mentorship room only)
Rule: "What do you really want? Are your current beliefs, decisions, and actions taking you there? The version of you who's already there, what's different about them?" (31:24) "When would be the most appropriate time for you to step into that version of yourself?" (32:08) "Which version of you has been in a better position? The one who feels fear and moves anyway, or the one who keeps delaying because it's easier?" (34:22) "So what do you need to let go of?" (34:28) "For someone to take a new action they first have to see themselves as the person who takes it." (30:45)
Why: "you're selling a different version of themselves." (30:35) The listener decides from the perceptual position of who they want to be.
Example: The parts frame (24:20): "it sounds like there's a part of you that's a bit scared ... you've got another part. The part that brought you to this call, the part that's hopeful, the part that wants more ... What would that version of you say to this version of you that's a little scared?"
Scopes: mentorship calls and workshops with DJs who asked for help
Conflict: this is an identity close. Refused entirely for couples (a couple choosing a DJ is not stepping into a new identity, and the sequence reads as pressure). In the mentorship room, the pressure lines are cut: "Do you really believe that? Or are you just trying to be nice?" (32:14) is refused; "why though?" stays
Status: adapted and restricted

## Refused from this source, with the reason

- **Authority tropes without a source.** At 17:50 Eli reaches for a stat ("80% of tennis matches are spent not hitting a single ball", then "studies have shown, research has proven, the debate is over") and admits he cannot verify it: "I can't do verbatim, damn you, Claude." That moment is the fabrication ban in action. No "studies show" without a source Joe can name.
- **"Now I just increased your dopamine."** (18:30) A claim about the listener's brain, used as authority. Not usable.
- **Consequence future pacing as pressure.** "so I can consequence future pace all of that" (24:06). Refused as a pressure tool; an honest cost question is already covered by the criteria-questions lesson.
- **"Do you really believe that, or are you just trying to be nice?"** (32:14) Refused; it accuses the listener of lying to get a stronger yes.
- **Labelling a room "victim story, complaining story"** (21:57). Refused as a verdict.
- **"I can take any bad answer and make it into a good answer."** (29:48) Refused where it means rewriting what the listener said.
- **Eli's own credentials and prices** ("nine figures", "100 million", course pricing). His facts, not Joe's. Never appear in Joe's mouth.
