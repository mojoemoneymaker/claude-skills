## From A (2024-07-31, Eli Wilde)

Lesson: Speak to one person
Rule: Address one listener at a time, never the room. After speaking to many, offer to talk one to one.
Why: The listener stays for what is said to them, not to a crowd.
Example: no example in source
Scopes: all
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: The two beliefs that create presence
Rule: Believe what you are saying is the most important thing right now, and that the listener is the most important person who could hear it.
Why: That belief is what reads as presence and connection.
Example: no example in source
Scopes: all live; teleprompter as pre-record state
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: Attention, Trust, Action
Rule: Capture attention, gain trust, then get them to take action, in that order.
Why: Each step is the precondition of the next.
Example: no example in source
Scopes: all
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: The Trust Trinity (first form)
Rule: The listener needs to trust the process, the product and themselves; sell the process first. They also need to trust the speaker.
Why: Trust in the method has to precede any offer built on it.
Example: no example in source
Scopes: sales, talks
Conflict: none in this form
Status: adopted (merged with the full form from B)

Lesson: Quality of life is quality of communication
Rule: Recorded as a governing idea, not a technique.
Status: not a lesson to apply; kept for context.

## From B, Eli Wilde section (2024-10-04)

Lesson: The speaker is a state inducer
Rule: Condition the emotion; align the room to one outcome; move the listener into the state where the next step is possible (thoughts, feelings, beliefs together).
Why: Decisions come from state.
Example: no example in source
Scopes: all
Conflict: guard against inducing state by pressure (FTC, no fake urgency)
Status: adopted with guard

Lesson: Patterns
Rule: Pattern recognition, pattern utilization, pattern creation.
Why: Attention goes to the novel against the familiar.
Example: no example in source
Scopes: reels, openers
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: Change the association
Rule: A change of belief is a change of meaning, a change of what the thing is associated with. Information does not change lives; experiences do. Thought, belief, meaning.
Why: The mechanism under every reframe.
Example: no example in source
Scopes: all
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: Repetition and intensity
Rule: A belief is fulfilled by repetition with intensity. Tell the same stories again.
Why: The ear needs the phrase more than once; feeling makes it stick.
Example: no example in source
Scopes: talks, teaching, reels
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: Tell the lesson inside a story
Rule: Put the lesson inside the story rather than after it.
Why: A conclusion the listener reaches is theirs.
Example: no example in source
Scopes: all
Conflict: none
Status: adopted

Lesson: The perfect strategy line
Rule: "You already have the perfect strategy, if you want to stay exactly where you are. But that's not why you're here, is it?" (Joe's rendering.)
Why: Reframes inaction as a chosen cost.
Example: as above, Joe's paraphrase
Scopes: teaching DJs who asked for help
Conflict: reads as pressure when said to a couple
Status: adapted, restricted to the mentorship room

Lesson: Teach as you give the testimonial
Rule: "Let me give you something you can do. This is what we did for ___. But this is for you."
Why: Proof and instruction in one move.
Example: as above, Joe's paraphrase
Scopes: teaching, talks, sales
Conflict: the example must be real and sourced (no fabrication)
Status: adopted with guard

Lesson: Closer composure
Rule: Be centred and calm.
Why: The listener reads state first.
Scopes: all live
Status: adopted (delivery.md)

Lesson: The Trust Trinity (full form)
Rule: Trust in you (your style: stagecraft; your story: story selling). Trust in themselves (the resistance: identity shift, pre-handle objections so they do not talk themselves into waiting; the reasons: an irresistible future). Trust in the product (the opportunity: buy in on the method before the offer, "you can't sell airline tickets if they don't trust planes", the method should clearly be the best for their goal; the offer: structured so it looks like the obvious choice, results in a desired outcome, positioned so they believe they will succeed).
Why: Any ask before all three are in place is a pitch.
Example: the airline line, Joe's paraphrase
Scopes: sales, talks
Conflict: "incentive for speed" and "speed up their time to the result" collide with no fake urgency and FTC; identity pressure collides with consultative rule
Status: adapted; the speed and identity-pressure parts refused

Lesson: First story is the expert story
Rule: The first story in a speech is how you went through something and came out with a system; people buy a system.
Why: Settles "why listen to you" before it is asked.
Scopes: talks, teaching
Status: adopted

Lesson: Reframes
Rule: Influence is changing the image in someone's mind. Types: challenge to opportunity, failure to feedback, problem to puzzle, pressure to privilege, fear to excitement, obstacle to stepping stone. Base template: "Most people are overly focused on ___. Now what we do is ___ and here's what that means ___." Write the reframed "evolution" of one or two common terms in your industry. After the reframe: "Can you see how this is different than what others have done in the past?"
Why: Replaces the old image with a new one and has the listener confirm it.
Example: Joe's own worked reframe of "DJ" (most brides just want the DJ to play music; what we do is the whole experience, which means no dull moment for you and your guests). Joe's words, usable as his.
Scopes: all
Conflict: none
Status: adopted (mechanism), template never pasted

Lesson: Criteria questions
Rule: Ask criteria questions rated one to ten; have them focus on the yes.
Scopes: sales, workshops
Conflict: none if used to understand, not to corner
Status: adapted

Lesson: Participation prompts
Rule: Fill in the blank, question control, raise your hand; creates group think and keeps engagement the whole time.
Scopes: teaching, workshops
Status: adopted

Lesson: Speaking fundamentals, state
Rule: If you cannot control your own state you cannot control the audience. Two keys: focus and breath. Four things to embody before going on: belief (certainty about what you are saying and about the audience; believe your thing is the thing), physiology (energy), outcome (feelings, beliefs, thoughts), rapport (trust and responsiveness; "you've already built this far, now ___"; vulnerability as quid pro quo, "Can I share something personal with you?", before the call or before the offer).
Scopes: all live
Status: adopted (delivery.md)

Lesson: Focus, breath, being yourself, presence, vocal modes, eye contact, charisma pattern, gestures, smile
Rule: Recorded in full in `delivery.md`. Summary: peripheral and foveal focus; get comfortable pausing, commas and full stops, practise reading aloud; be ordinary and extraordinary, natural, show imperfection, a quick funny story; stance rules; Sage, Warrior, Lover with vocal variety as the heartbeat; charisma pattern Sage then Warrior then Lover; gesture set per mode plus the Sovereign L; eye rules (peripherals, no sprinkler or laser, pause and look, always to one, smile with the eyes, random selection, every line is a gift); slight smile with the "I know something you don't", "I care about you", "I want to have fun with you" feeling.
Scopes: all live, and delivery notes for teleprompter reads
Status: adopted

Lesson: ATA
Rule: Attention, Trust, Action.
Status: adopted (same as A)
