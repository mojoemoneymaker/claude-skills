# Delivery lessons

The lessons this skill applies on its own. Everything about WHAT is said, how a belief is
changed, how a piece is ordered, how a question carries a reader (reframes, two sales, question
control, bridge questions, tie-downs, attention generators, the through line, the story arc)
lives in the `belief-patterns` skill (`references/patterns.md`). Run that first. This file is
the layer for how it sounds when a person says it. A lesson lives in exactly one of the two.

Sources: Joe's notes from Eli Wilde's live events (2024-07-31, 2024-10-04, 2024-10-05), the NLP
3.0 flashcards deck in Joe's Drive, and the speaker drills video (YouTube `dDCdGJ_TIZ0`, the
only source with verbatim quotes, timestamped). Full records in
`belief-patterns/references/sources/`.

**Speak to one person.** Never address the room. Every line is aimed at one listener, and the
eyes and the words pick one person at a time. Why: a line said to a crowd lands on nobody; a
line said to one person is felt by everyone. Scopes: all, and the MC scope most of all, where
the temptation to shout "everybody" is strongest. Status: adopted. Source: EW-notes-2024-07-31,
EW-notes-2024-10-04 (eye contact: "never speak to many, always speak to one"). KB: Principle:
Speak to one person, never to the room.

**Believe it is the most important thing, for the most important person.** Before speaking,
hold two beliefs: what is being said is the most important thing right now, and the person
hearing it is the most important person who could hear it. Why: that state is what the listener
reads as presence, and presence is what makes them stay. Scopes: all live moments; for a
teleprompter read it is the state Joe steps into before pressing record. Status: adopted.
Source: EW-notes-2024-07-31. KB: Principle: The two beliefs that create presence.

**Participation prompts keep a room in the talk.** Fill in the blank, a question the room
answers, a raised hand, a one-to-ten rating. Why: a listener who has answered something is
part of the talk, and group answers create group agreement. Scopes: teaching, workshops,
mentorship calls; in a reel the equivalent is a direct question the viewer answers in their
head. Status: adopted. Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04. KB: Tactic: Participation prompts.

**Call and response.** Ask the room to say the key word back. "What's it called? Question
control." (11:14) "What is it? Say it again. Action." (14:31) Why: the key word said by the room
is the key word the room remembers; it is repetition done by the listener. Scopes: teaching,
workshops, mentorship calls. In a reel the viewer answers in their head, so ask once and say
the word once, plainly. Status: adopted. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Tactic: Call and response.

**It's a rhythm, and Joe's will sound like Joe.** "It's a rhythm. And how you do the same
thing, it'll sound different from you. And you'll ask yourself questions, and it just breaks up
the monotony." (15:19) Why: the pattern is the skeleton; the voice is the speaker's, and a
copied phrasing sounds copied. A device that fits Joe: the self-quote. "Somebody once told me
that if I want to be successful, I need to start speaking. And I thought, is that true?"
(15:52) Scopes: all. Status: adopted. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Principle: It's a rhythm.

**Be ordinary and extraordinary.** Be natural, show one imperfection, tell one quick funny
story. Why: a speaker with no flaws is not trusted because he is not believed. Scopes: all live
moments and reels. Status: adopted. Guard: the imperfection is a real one Joe has told before;
never invented for effect. Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04 (Being Yourself). KB: Principle: Be
ordinary and extraordinary.

**Words carry the logic, tone carries the feeling.** A belief is installed by both. Conviction
corrects tonality: a speaker who believes the line will say it right. Why: the listener
believes the tone before the words. Scopes: all live moments; for a teleprompter read it is
the reason delivery notes exist. Status: adopted. Source: NLP-3.0-cards (cards 57, 94),
EW-notes-2024-10-04 (closer composure). KB: Principle: Words carry logic, tone carries feeling.

**The close is a continuation of the lesson.** The `wdjm-script-writer` CTA rules and the
offer-state CTA rule in `process-transcript` govern the words. This skill adds one thing:
the last line is said in the Lover mode (`delivery.md`), slow, still, and to one person. Why:
an invitation said as an instruction is refused. Scopes: reels, talks, calls. Status: adopted.
Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04 (charisma pattern ends in Lover). KB: not filed separately; part
of the charisma pattern entry.

The mechanics of delivery (state before speaking, breath, presence, the Sage, Warrior and Lover
modes, eyes, gestures, face) are in `delivery.md`. The read-aloud checklist is in
