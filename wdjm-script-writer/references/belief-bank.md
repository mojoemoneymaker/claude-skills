# Belief Bank

A starting library of beliefs to shift, organized by category. These are seeds, not limits — generate new beliefs in the same shape when the audience or angle calls for it.

## How to use this bank

1. **Match belief to bottleneck.** Don't pick the most interesting belief — pick the one the audience actually holds right now and is stuck on.
2. **Tag the belief** with its category before drafting. The category informs the story mode (see `story-modes.md`).
3. **Prefer contrarian versions** when they sharpen the reframe without becoming fake controversy. Every contrarian belief in this bank can be paired with a non-contrarian version of the same idea — pick the one that lands better for the specific viewer.

## Belief categories

Tag every belief as one of these:

- **possibility** — *this is achievable for someone like me*
- **problem diagnosis** — *the thing keeping me stuck is X (and it's not what I thought)*
- **mechanism** — *the way change actually happens is Y*
- **value** — *what I produce is worth more than I'm pricing it at*
- **identity** — *who I'm becoming, not just what I'm doing*
- **urgency** — *waiting has a cost I'm not counting*
- **process** — *here's how the work actually gets done*
- **content** — *how visibility and trust get built online*
- **sales** — *how buying decisions actually happen*
- **MC / performance** — *what makes the live work great*
- **backend / business** — *the systems behind the artistry*
- **mentor** — *why guided reps beat solo trial-and-error*
- **contrarian** — *the obvious answer is wrong* (can overlap with any category above)

## Seed beliefs

### Possibility

- This is possible for someone like me.
- I do not need a huge company to charge more.
- I do not need to be the loudest DJ in my market to become the most trusted.
- I can become more premium without becoming fake or salesy.

### Problem diagnosis

- My real bottleneck is probably not gear.
- My problem may not be leads. It may be trust, positioning, or sales skill.
- If I am stuck, the issue is probably not effort. It is structure, direction, or feedback.
- Being talented is not enough if I am invisible online.
- Confidence problems are often structure problems in disguise.
- Inconsistency is often a systems problem, not a motivation problem.

### Value

- The more problems I solve, the more valuable I become.
- Couples are not just paying for music. They are paying for confidence, flow, and leadership.
- MC skill is part of the value, not an optional extra.
- Backend systems increase perceived professionalism.
- Simplicity can feel more premium than a pile of options.

### Sales

- Couples buy based on how they feel, then justify it with logic.
- The sales call should diagnose and lead, not chase and explain.
- Premium pricing is tied to outcomes, not line items.
- Guest count does not reduce the value of the outcome being created.
- Simplicity sells better than menu-style confusion.
- Good content should do most of the selling before the call.

### Content

- Visibility beats talent online.
- Content builds trust before the first conversation.
- The goal is not virality. The goal is relevance.
- A small video seen by the right people can outperform a viral one seen by the wrong people.
- The barrier is rarely gear. It is usually the lack of a system.
- Done beats perfect.
- Specific content outperforms generic content.
- Content that serves the couple outperforms content that promotes the DJ.

### MC / Performance

- Confidence comes from structure, not personality alone.
- Great MCing is structured flexibility, not robotic scripting.
- Guests need clarity and guidance more than clever phrasing.
- Reading the room matters more than sounding polished in a vacuum.
- A great MC is an extension of the couple, not just an announcer.

### Mentor / Urgency

- Trial and error is more expensive than it looks.
- A mentor can compress years of drifting into weeks of focused reps.
- If I wait for perfect timing, I will probably stay in the same place.
- Another wedding season at the same level has a cost.
- Progress accelerates when someone can spot the bottleneck faster than you can.

## Contrarian belief formula

When a belief in the bank has an obvious "common wisdom" version, build the contrarian counterpart:

1. **Old belief** — what they currently think (and why it feels true)
2. **Why it feels true** — name the surface-level evidence
3. **What makes it incomplete** — the missing piece
4. **New belief** — the reframe
5. **Practical implication** — what they do differently

**Example:**
- Old belief: *I need better gear to charge more.*
- Why it feels true: *Gear is visible and easy to compare.*
- What makes it incomplete: *Couples don't pay most for visible equipment alone.*
- New belief: *Pricing power comes more from trust, guidance, and solved problems.*
- Practical implication: *Improve consults, MC skill, flow management, and backend before upgrading gear.*

A contrarian belief is only worth using when it's grounded in real experience, creates a clearer reframe than the non-contrarian version, helps the viewer see themselves more accurately, doesn't slip into fake controversy, and still sounds like Joseph.

## Audience slice options (for narrowing further)

When the default audience-of-one isn't sharp enough, narrow to one of these:

- The underpriced DJ
- The part-time DJ with a day job
- The DJ who wants better consults
- The DJ with weak MC confidence
- The DJ stuck on content consistency
- The DJ who feels invisible online
- The DJ who gets inquiries but weak closes
- The DJ who wants a more premium brand
- The DJ who is good technically but weak strategically

Pick the slice that matches the belief — a belief about consult skill writes naturally to "the DJ who wants better consults," not to the general WDJM audience.
