# Hook Bank

Proven hook patterns for short-form scripts. **Use these as templates, not as copy.** A hook lifted verbatim sounds like a hook lifted verbatim — fill in the variable, then read it back and rewrite anything that feels generic.

## The four standards (every hook must pass)

1. **Clarity** — viewer knows the subject within the first sentence
2. **Relevance** — the *right* viewer feels called out immediately
3. **Curiosity** — one clear open loop (not three)
4. **Speed to value** — value starts in sentence one, not sentence three

## Single-subject, single-question rule

A hook does one thing. If it's trying to:
- introduce the audience *and* tease the answer *and* establish credibility — cut two of them
- ask two questions — pick the sharper one
- name three problems — name one

The other things have room in the body. Don't try to win the whole script in the hook.

## Length and shape

- **1 to 3 sentences.** Three is the ceiling, not the target.
- **First sentence establishes topic clarity.** No mystery openers like "I learned something wild" — name the topic.
- **Use "you" early.** Often in sentence one.
- **Active voice.** Short words.
- **Contrast words = your friend:** *but, actually, instead, turns out, yet, except.* These do most of the curiosity work without sounding gimmicky.

## Hook patterns by type

### Audience Callout

Names the viewer first so the right person stays.

- *If you're a solo wedding DJ stuck under [price point], this is for you.*
- *If you're part-time and trying to grow without sounding fake, listen to this.*
- *Wedding DJs: if you're still solving this the obvious way, hear me out.*
- *If you've been DJing for years and your rate hasn't moved, this is the conversation.*

### Problem Diagnosis

Names a problem they have but misread.

- *The thing keeping most wedding DJs stuck is not what they think.*
- *Most DJs think the bottleneck is [obvious thing]. It usually isn't.*
- *If you're trying to fix this with [wrong fix], that might be the real issue.*
- *You probably don't have a [obvious problem] problem. You have a [real problem] problem.*

### Contrarian

Inverts the assumption they walked in with.

- *The upgrade you need probably isn't in a road case.*
- *The real reason you're stuck might have nothing to do with talent.*
- *Raising your rate is not where the work starts. It starts earlier than that.*
- *[Common advice] is actually what's keeping a lot of DJs underpriced.*

### Story

Pulls the viewer into Joseph's own arc.

- *I used to think [old belief]. Here's what changed for me.*
- *I learned this the hard way after [specific context].*
- *Early on, I thought [common assumption]. I was wrong.*
- *The week I figured this out, my pricing made sense for the first time.*

### Urgency

Names the cost of staying the same — without manufactured panic.

- *If you've been saying "someday," be careful.*
- *Another season at the same level has a cost most DJs never calculate.*
- *Trial and error feels cheaper until you count the time.*
- *The cost of staying where you are is usually invisible until it isn't.*

### Mentor

Reframes what they actually need.

- *I didn't need more information. I needed better feedback.*
- *Most DJs do not need another tip. They need correction.*
- *What speeds this up is not motivation. It is guided reps.*
- *You probably don't have a learning problem. You have a feedback problem.*

## Hook anti-patterns

Don't ship these:

- **Vague bait** — *"This will change everything you thought about DJing."* (Says nothing.)
- **Delayed context** — *"I want to tell you about something I've been thinking about lately…"* (Get to the topic.)
- **Too many ideas at once** — *"If you're a wedding DJ, or a DJ trying to break in, or someone who runs events…"* (Pick one.)
- **No audience callout** — generic openers that any niche could use.
- **Mystery-over-relevance** — *"I'm not going to tell you what this is yet."* (You have 3 seconds; relevance wins over suspense.)
- **Too much "I" in the hook** — make the hook about *them*, not you.

## Quick test

Read the hook out loud. If, after sentence one, you can't tell:
- who this is for, **and**
- what the video is about, **and**
- why they should keep watching —

…the hook isn't done yet. Rewrite.
