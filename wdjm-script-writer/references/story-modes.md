# Story Modes

The body of every script follows one of these seven modes. Pick **one mode per script**, don't blend. The mode is chosen by matching it to the *belief type* you're shifting.

## How to pick

| Belief type | Best modes |
|---|---|
| Possibility belief | Story, Identity Shift |
| Problem diagnosis | Diagnostic, Contrarian Reframe |
| Mechanism belief | Principles, Diagnostic |
| Value belief | Myth Breaker, Principles |
| Identity belief | Identity Shift, Story |
| Urgency belief | Cost of Inaction |
| Process belief | Principles, Diagnostic |
| Content belief | Myth Breaker, Principles |
| Sales belief | Diagnostic, Contrarian Reframe |
| MC/performance | Principles, Story |
| Mentor belief | Cost of Inaction, Story |
| Contrarian belief | Contrarian Reframe |

When two modes both fit, pick the one whose proof is strongest for this specific belief.

## Mode A: Story

Joseph's own arc. Best when the lesson lands harder coming from lived experience.

**Structure:**
1. **Before**, where I was, what I believed, what wasn't working
2. **Mistake**, what I kept doing because I believed the wrong thing
3. **Realization**, the moment or insight that shifted it
4. **After**, what changed when the new belief took hold

Keep the *before* short. The realization is the payload.

## Mode B: Principles

A small number of clear rules. Best when the belief is mechanical (*here's how X actually works*).

**Structure:**
- 2 to 3 principles
- 2 to 3 contrasts (this, not that)
- 2 to 3 practical rules

Don't number past three. If you have four, one of them is hiding inside another.

## Mode C: Contrarian Reframe

The five-beat contrarian formula. Use when the obvious answer is wrong and you can prove it.

**Structure:**
1. **Old belief**, what they currently think
2. **Why it feels true**, the surface-level evidence
3. **What makes it incomplete**, the missing piece
4. **New belief**, the reframe
5. **Practical implication**, what they do differently

Steps 2 and 3 are non-negotiable. Without them, the contrarian sounds like contrarianism for sport.

## Mode D: Myth Breaker

A specific assumption, dismantled. Lighter than Contrarian Reframe, more about correcting received wisdom than fully inverting it.

**Structure:**
1. **Assumption**, what DJs commonly believe
2. **Why DJs believe it**, where the assumption comes from
3. **What reality rewards instead**, what actually works
4. **Takeaway**, the rule that replaces the myth

## Mode E: Diagnostic

A symptom, traced to its real cause. Best when the audience knows something's wrong but has misdiagnosed it.

**Structure:**
1. **Symptom**, the visible problem
2. **Hidden cause**, what's actually driving it
3. **Proof**, specific detail or example showing the link
4. **Next move**, the first action that addresses the real cause

This mode rewards specificity. Vague symptoms produce vague diagnoses.

## Mode F: Cost of Inaction

What compounds if nothing changes. Use for urgency beliefs, but earn the urgency, don't manufacture it.

**Structure:**
1. **Current pattern**, what they're doing now
2. **Hidden cost**, what it's costing that they're not counting
3. **What compounds if unchanged**, the trajectory
4. **Better path**, what changes when the pattern breaks

Honest urgency, never fake urgency. If you can't name a real cost, this isn't the right mode.

## Mode G: Identity Shift

Reframes who they're being, not just what they're doing. Use sparingly, when it lands, it lands hard; when it's forced, it sounds like a TED talk.

**Structure:**
1. **Who they think they are now**, the current identity
2. **What that identity produces**, the natural outcomes
3. **Who they need to become**, the next identity
4. **What changes when that identity shifts**, the new outcomes

The shift has to be specific and earned. "Become a premium DJ" is not an identity shift. "Stop thinking of yourself as the person they hire and start thinking of yourself as the person they trust to run the night", that is.

## Mode anti-patterns

- **Blending two modes**: *"Let me tell you a story… and here are three principles…"* Pick one.
- **Skipping the proof beat**: every mode has a step where you ground the belief in something specific. Don't drop it.
- **Wrong mode for the belief**: a sales belief in Identity Shift mode feels off; an identity belief in Principles mode feels flat. The table above is there for a reason.
