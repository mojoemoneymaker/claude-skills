# Voice, Proof, and Trust Anchors

This file governs *how a script sounds* and *what makes it credible*. Load it before finalizing any draft.

## Brand voice: DJ MOJOE

Joseph sounds like:

- **Calm authority**: confident without proving it
- **Practical intelligence**: useful before clever
- **Honest mentorship**: corrects without scolding
- **Measured confidence**: claims only what's earned
- **Lived experience**: talks from reps, not theory
- **Principle-based teaching**: rules over tips
- **Conversational clarity**: sounds like one person talking to one person
- **Peer-level relatability**: never lectures down

He is **not**:

- Hypey
- Guru-coded
- Manipulative
- Fake-urgent
- Overly prescriptive
- Loud for attention's sake

### Voice markers: use often

These phrases carry the voice naturally. Sprinkle them in, don't overuse them.

- *In my experience…*
- *What I've found…*
- *What I've noticed…*
- *Here's what most DJs miss…*
- *The shift for me was…*
- *What changed for me…*
- *Ask yourself…*
- *The goal isn't perfection. It's consistency.*

### Voice markers: never use

These break the voice on contact:

- guaranteed
- crush it
- dominate
- game changer
- secret sauce
- six figures
- passive income
- only X spots left
- this will change your life
- any fake scarcity
- any fake certainty
- any unverified student claim

## Language clarity standards

Use **atomic messaging**:

- Simpler words
- Fewer words
- Active voice
- One idea per line
- Restate complex ideas in simpler language

**Target reading level:** 5th to 7th grade clarity, written with adult respect, in a natural spoken rhythm. Simple is not simplistic, simple is clear.

## Trust Anchor Rule

**Include exactly one trust anchor per script. Never stack.**

A trust anchor is a single, specific credibility marker that tells the viewer *why they should listen to this person.* One per script, picked to match the belief being shifted.

### Approved trust anchors

| Anchor | Best paired with beliefs about |
|---|---|
| *I've DJ'd over 300 weddings in Southern California.* | mechanism, MC/performance, problem diagnosis |
| *I raised my fee from $2.5k to $6k per wedding and stayed booked.* | pricing, value, sales, premium positioning |
| *We've got 250+ DJs inside the Wedding DJ Mastery community.* | possibility, mentor, social proof |
| *Inside the Wedding DJ Mastery Accelerator, we work through this step with you live over 8 weeks.* | process, mentor, urgency (works well as CTA bridge) |

Pick the one that the belief earns. If the script is about pricing power, the rate-progression anchor is sharper than the wedding-count anchor.

## Proof Bank

Use **one main proof type per script** unless the user asks for more.

### Founder credentials

- 300+ weddings in Southern California
- Current premium rate
- Long-term active practitioner (not retired teacher)

### Rate progression proof

- $2.5k → $4k → $5k → $6k while staying booked
- Specific because it's specific, don't round it or generalize it

### Content proof

- Venue-specific long-form content with modest views but real bookings
- Hip-hop to Spanish transition Reel that built trust and led to a booking
- A cappella end-of-night Reel that demonstrated adaptability and led to a booking

### Program proof

- 250+ DJs in community
- Live 8-week work-through format

### Wedding logic proof

These are framing proofs, *why* the belief is true, drawn from how couples actually decide:

- Couples want fewer unknowns.
- Couples want someone who can guide, host, manage flow, and reduce stress.
- Premium couples often buy confidence and clarity, not just equipment.

## Trust + proof composition rule

A script needs:
- exactly **one trust anchor** (who's speaking and why they're credible), and
- exactly **one main proof type** (the evidence for the belief itself)

These are two different things. Don't conflate them. The trust anchor is *credentialing*; the proof is *the evidence inside the argument*.

## Honesty guardrails

- Never invent student outcomes.
- Never round numbers up.
- Never claim a result you can't point to.
- Never replace persuasion with posture.
- When using *"in my experience"* or *"I've noticed"*, actually be drawing on Joseph's experience. If you're not sure he'd say it, ask before writing it.
