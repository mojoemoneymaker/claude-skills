# belief-patterns

One home for the persuasion patterns. Every skill that writes something meant to change a mind
points here instead of restating them, because a pattern copied into five skills drifts into
five patterns. This skill decides how a piece is structured to be believed: what the reader has
to accept first, how the belief turns, how the reader is carried from point to point, and where
the ask sits. The content skill for the lane decides the format. `spoken-script` decides how it
sounds when said. `joe-copy-standards` and the project copy law decide what is allowed, and
they win every conflict.

## Before applying a pattern

Read `joe-copy-standards` (Dropbox `AI Operating System/skills/`). Two of its laws fail
persuasion drafts most often: never name a doubt the reader did not raise in order to deny it
(rule 9), and never invent a story, stat, name or outcome to make a pattern land (rule 5). A
reframe with a made-up example is a lie with good structure. Where a pattern needs proof Joe
has not supplied, leave a bracketed slot and say so.

## The five jobs

A reader or listener does five things, in order, and every pattern serves one of them:

1. **Pay attention.** Question control, bridge questions, attention generators, the one-word
   thesis, the expert story.
2. **Trust the speaker.** The Trust Trinity's first leg, describe their problem better than they
   can, label and mirror, seek to understand, wrap the question, demonstrate the gap.
3. **Take on one belief.** Two sales (process before product), the through line, the reframe,
   change the association, tell the lesson inside a story, repetition and intensity, linking
   phrases, three layers of pain, frames as focus and meaning, the story arc.
4. **Follow and understand.** Four levels of teaching, unique-relatable-applicable-directed,
   teach as you give the testimonial, tie-downs, reveal levels.
5. **Move to action.** Attention-Trust-Action, trust themselves, criteria questions, the
   version-of-you sequence (mentorship room only).

The order is the argument. A close before trust is a pitch. A reframe before attention is
noise. Two sales out of order (product before process) is the crypto software pitched to someone
who does not believe in crypto.

## Mode A: structure a piece

1. **Name the lane, the reader, and the one belief.** Which lane (`lanes.md`), which one
   person is reading or listening, and what they must believe afterwards that they do not
   believe now. Two beliefs means two pieces.
2. **Order the two sales.** What must the reader accept about the process before the product
   makes sense? Put that first, and make the product depend on it. For a couple: the reception
   is run by one voice, and who that voice is decides the night; the DJ comes second. For a DJ:
   a Core 6 belief from the Belief and Offer Foundation; the Accelerator comes second.
3. **Pick the patterns per job**, no more than one or two per job for a short piece. Apply the
   mechanism written in `patterns.md`, not the surface. A reframe that reads as the template is
   recitation ("money aside, money aside" in the source), and recitation changes no belief.
4. **Carry the reader.** Every hard cut between points becomes a bridge question or an
   attention generator. In writing, the bridge is the question-phrased subhead. In speech, it
   is said out loud. Tie-downs only in the spoken lanes.
5. **Check the guards.** Every reframe connected to what the reader said or already believes.
   No doubt named that the reader did not raise. No urgency the offer state does not support
   (`process-transcript` carries the current CTA rule). Every story, number and name traced to
   Joe, the registry, or a bracketed slot. Anything from `refused.md`: out.
6. **Hand off.** The content skill for the lane writes the format. If the piece is read aloud,
   `spoken-script` runs last.

Deliver as a draft. Name which patterns carried the piece in one line above it, so Joe can see
the structure without reading for it.

## Mode B: ingest a source

Triggered by "add this to the patterns", "learn from this speaker", "train you on this", or a
new transcript. Follow `references/sources/README.md`; the short version: do it once (registry
and Knowledge Base check), read it all (delegate if large), extract in the fixed shape with
verbatim examples, file in the Notion Knowledge Base (Source = the teacher, Status = New),
write the source record, merge into `patterns.md` or route delivery lessons to `spoken-script`,
record refusals with reasons, report as a draft. Joe's laws are not up for a vote by a new
teacher.

## Composition rule

`belief-patterns` first, the lane's content skill for format, `spoken-script` last if it is
said aloud, `joe-copy-standards` and the project copy law over all of it. This skill never
introduces a fact. Attribution is honest: verbatim lines carry timestamps, Joe's notes are
Joe's paraphrase, Joe's own lines are marked as his.

## Quality pass

- One belief. The reader could say it back in one sentence.
- Process before product. The offer depends on something the reader accepted earlier in the
  piece.
- Every reframe connected to a belief the reader holds, written fresh, followed by a
  confirming question where the lane allows one.
- No hard cuts. Each transition is a bridge question, an attention generator, or a
  question-phrased subhead.
- No doubt planted. No urgency the offer cannot carry. No refused technique.
- Every proof traced or slotted. Zero dashes, zero banned words, checker run
  (`spoken-script/scripts/read_aloud_check.py` works on any markdown).
- No first-person story, observation or "in my experience" claim that Joe did not supply. A
  sentence that starts "I used to" or "I've watched couples" is a fact about Joe, and an
  invented one goes out under his name. If the piece needs it, write the bracketed slot and say
  what it needs to contain.
- The first sale never names the seller. A section, post or opener that exists to install the
  process belief does not say "I", "Joe" or "DJ MOJOE" until the second sale begins.
- A CTA never names the offer that is on waitlist, not even to contrast it with the free room.
  "Open right now, no waitlist" is a scarcity cue in a nicer coat.
- Labelled DRAFT, patterns named above it.

## Anti-patterns

- The template with the blanks filled in.
- The product before the process.
- Three beliefs in one piece.
- A bridge question that has no answer in the next sentence.
- A tie-down on a page.
- Naming the doubt to deny it.
- A first-person anecdote Joe did not supply.
- Naming the waitlisted offer in a CTA.
- Any technique from `refused.md`, however well it worked in the source.
