# Patterns

The persuasion structure this skill applies, grouped by the job the reader or listener is doing
at that moment. Every pattern names its source and its adoption status:

- **adopted**: apply as taught.
- **adapted**: the mechanism is kept, the surface is changed to fit Joe's rules or his rooms.
- **not adopted**: recorded in `refused.md` so the skill knows what it refuses and why.

Attribution is exact. "Joe's notes" means Joe's own paraphrase of a live event, not the
teacher's words. Lines in quotation marks with a timestamp are verbatim from the speaker drills
video (EW-drills-yt); everything else is paraphrase. Each pattern names its Knowledge Base entry
so any other thread can retrieve it from Notion (Source = Eli Wilde).

Delivery-only lessons (how a line is said: presence, state, breath, vocal modes, eyes, gestures,
rhythm in the mouth) live in `spoken-script`, not here. A pattern lives in exactly one skill.

## 1. Pay attention

The listener gives the speaker a few seconds. These lessons decide whether the next minute
is earned.

**The speaker is a state inducer.** The job is not to deliver information; it is to move the
listener from their current state into the state where the next step is possible (the notes
call it a buying state: thoughts, feelings, beliefs aligned). Why: people act from state, not
from facts. "All decisions are state decisions." Scopes: all. Status: adopted, with one guard:
the state is induced by conviction and story, never by pressure or manufactured urgency
(`joe-copy-standards`, FTC rule). Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04, EW-notes-2024-10-05. KB:
Principle: The speaker is a state inducer.

**Open with the expert story.** The first story in a talk is the one where the speaker went
through the thing and came out with a system, because people buy a system, not a person. Why:
it settles the question "why should I listen to you" before the listener has to ask it. Scopes:
talks, teaching segments, long reels; adapted for a sales call, where the expert story is one
sentence, not a story. Status: adopted. Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04. KB: Framework: The
expert story opens the talk.

**Hooks are pattern work.** Recognise the pattern the listener expects, use it, then break or
create one. Why: attention goes to what is novel against a background of the familiar. Scopes:
reels and talk openers. The `wdjm-script-writer` hook bank already carries Joe's proven hook
patterns; this lesson explains why they work, it does not replace them. Status: adopted.
Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04 (patterns: recognition, utilization, creation). KB: Principle:
Attention is pattern work.

**Question control: interrogate your own content.** Make a statement, then immediately ask
yourself the question a listener would ask about it, then answer it. The answer is the next
statement. "I said a statement and then I immediately asked myself a question about the
statement I just made. And that sets up my next statement." (10:39) Why: four statements in a
row go flat ("It started to get boring, yeah?" 10:33); a self-question breaks the monotony and
"it's more believable ... they have less uncertainty if I interrogate my own content." (13:24)
The drill: take any sentence, find the question that goes before it and the question that goes
after it. Scopes: teaching, talks, reels, and sales calls, where the self-question replaces a
monologue. Status: adopted. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Tactic: Question control.

**Attention generators.** Spoken signposts that reset attention before a point, from the
session verbatim: "Here's what you need to remember. Bottom line is this. Here's the most
important thing you want to think about. Here's why it's so important. Here's the crazy part.
Check this out. Here's the fun part." (18:17) Why: "80% of influence is focus, it's controlling
focus, and people's focus begins to wane." (18:38) The two questions in every listener's mind:
"can I trust this person, and can I listen to this person." (20:05) "And it's not your fault.
It's the speaker's fault." (20:24) Scopes: all; in a reel these are the rehooks, and the
`wdjm-script-writer` rehook rule carries Joe's own versions. Guard: "this might shock you"
earns its place only when what follows would in fact surprise the listener. Status: adopted.
Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Script/Language: Attention generators.

**The thesis pattern: one word, then why, then what it means.** A provocative claim that lands
on one word, a self-question why, the answer, a self-question what that means, the
implication. "By far, the number one most common thing holding people back from getting the
life they want comes down to one word, action. So why is action the number one thing holding
people back?" (14:00) "So what does it mean to take action? It means..." (15:07) Why: it is
question control applied to a whole point, and it hands the listener the one word to hold.
Scopes: teaching openers, reels, talks. Status: adopted. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Framework:
The thesis pattern.

## 2. Trust the speaker

Attention without trust is a stranger talking. These lessons are the Trust Trinity's first
leg (trust in the speaker) and the rapport work around it.

**The Trust Trinity.** The listener must trust three things, in order: the speaker (their style
and their story), themselves (that they can do it, and that the future is worth it), and the
product (the method first, then the offer). Sell the method before the offer: nobody buys a
plane ticket from an airline if they do not trust planes. Why: an ask made before all three are
in place is felt as a pitch. Scopes: sales talk tracks and talks that end in an invitation;
for a couple the "product" is the approach to the reception, and the "method" is why a DJ who
runs the night differs from one who plays a playlist. Status: adapted. The notes also record
"the opportunity should have incentive for speed"; that part is not adopted (see section 6).
Source: EW-notes-2024-07-31, EW-notes-2024-10-04. KB: Framework: The Trust Trinity.

**Vulnerability is quid pro quo, and it is something already healed.** Ask permission ("Can I
share something personal with you?"), give a little, and the listener gives a little back.
Vulnerability is sharing something you have healed from, not something you are bleeding from.
Why: rapport is built by exchange, and a healed story carries authority where an open wound
carries only sympathy. Scopes: sales calls, teaching, long-form. Status: adopted. Source:
EW-notes-2024-10-04 (rapport), TW morning session for the "healed" definition. KB: Tactic:
Vulnerability as quid pro quo.

**Authority comes from what could not be tolerated.** The things a speaker has faced, wrestled
with and refused to tolerate are the only things they carry authority on. Why: the listener
grants authority to overcoming, not to knowing. Scopes: talks, teaching, reels built on Joe's
own arc. Status: adopted. Source: TW morning session, EW-notes-2024-10-04. KB: Principle:
Authority comes from what you would not tolerate.

**Describe their problem better than they can.** If the speaker names the listener's situation
more precisely than the listener could, the listener assumes the speaker has the solution.
Why: precision about the problem is the fastest proof of expertise. Scopes: reels (the setup
beat), sales calls (the first questions), teaching openers. Status: adopted. Source:
NLP-3.0-cards (card 48). KB: Principle: Describe their problem better than they can.

**Label the emotion, mirror the words.** When the listener describes something, name the
feeling under it ("it sounds like that's been stressful") and reflect their key words back as a
question ("struggling to get quality leads?"). Why: being seen between the lines builds rapport
faster than any credential, and a mirrored phrase invites the listener to go deeper. Scopes:
sales calls, mentorship calls, any live exchange. Status: adopted. Source: NLP-3.0-cards
(cards 16, 39, 118). KB: Tactic: Label the emotion, mirror the words.

**Seek to understand, not to argue.** Be curious about why the listener thinks what they think.
Interrupting is rude when it turns attention to the speaker and fine when it turns attention
back to the listener. "Yes, and" never "yes, but". Why: a listener who feels argued with
defends; a listener who feels understood explores. Scopes: sales calls, objection responses.
Status: adopted. Source: NLP-3.0-cards (cards 44, 86, 92). KB: Principle: Seek to understand,
not to argue.

**Demonstrate the gap, do not describe it.** "I pull people on stage and I speak better than
them most of the time. So they see there's a gap and they like it and there's a
demonstration." (05:07) Why: a shown skill is believed, a claimed skill is doubted. Scopes:
talks and workshops; for Joe's reels the demonstration is the footage (the a cappella end of
night, the hip-hop to Spanish transition), never a claim about it. Adapted so the demonstration
never humiliates a participant. Status: adapted. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Principle:
Demonstrate the gap.

**Wrap the question: acknowledge before you move on.** Answer what they said before asking
the next thing. "I totally get that, and the fact that you're wanting to improve this already
puts you ahead of so many people." (28:08) "I didn't just go to the next question. How long have
you had this problem? A year? Okay. And how's that been impacted on you? ... it jumps ahead too
much. It's the wrapping of the question." (28:29) Why: the listener is rewarded for saying
something true and goes further next time. Scopes: sales calls, mentorship calls, any
interview. Guard: the source also says "I can take any bad answer and make it into a good
answer" (29:48). Reflect what they said and name what is useful in it; never rewrite it, and
never for a couple. Status: adapted. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Tactic: Wrap the question.

## 3. Take on one belief

Trust earned, the speaker gets to change one thing the listener believes. One.

**Change the association, and the meaning changes.** A belief is a meaning attached to an
experience. Information does not change lives, experiences do; so the work is to change what
the listener associates the thing with, through a story they feel, not a fact they hear. Why:
this is the mechanism under every reframe. Scopes: all. Status: adopted. Source:
EW-notes-2024-10-04 (transformation, thought, belief, meaning), NLP-3.0-cards (card 91). KB:
Principle: Change the association and the meaning changes.

**Tell the lesson inside a story.** Never state the principle and then illustrate it. Put the
principle inside the story so the listener arrives at it themselves. Why: a conclusion the
listener reached is theirs; a conclusion the speaker stated is the speaker's. Scopes: all;
this is also WDJM standard 14 and the belief installation method (story plus contrast) in the
Knowledge Resources hub, which this lesson does not restate. Status: adopted. Source:
EW-notes-2024-10-04. KB: Principle: Tell the lesson inside a story.

**Repetition and intensity install a belief.** A belief is fulfilled by hearing it again, in the
same words, with feeling behind it. Why: the ear needs the phrase more than once, and the
feeling is what makes it stick. Scopes: talks, teaching, long reels (the key phrase returns at
the turn and at the close); in a thirty-second reel, once at the turn and once at the end.
Status: adopted. Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04. KB: Principle: Repetition and intensity.

**The reframe: it's not that, it's this, and here's what that means.** Influence is changing the
image in the listener's mind. The base shape: "Most people are overly focused on X. What we do
is Y, and here's what that means for you: Z." Types of reframe worth reaching for: challenge to
opportunity, failure to feedback, problem to puzzle, pressure to privilege, fear to excitement,
obstacle to stepping stone. Joe's own worked example from the notes, for "DJ": most couples
think the DJ's job is the music; what Joe does is run the whole experience, which means there
is never a dead moment for them or their guests. After the reframe, ask: "Can you see how this
is different from what you've seen before?" Why: the reframe replaces the old image with a new
one and the question makes the listener confirm it in their own words. Scopes: all. Status:
adopted for the mechanism; the template is a shape to write from, never a line to paste
(see SKILL.md anti-patterns). Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04, NLP-3.0-cards (card 10, 87, 88).
KB: Framework: The reframe.

**Link every feature to what it means for them.** Linking phrases: "so that", "in order to",
"which allows you to", "which means", "and what that means for you is". Why: the listener does
not want the thing, they want what the thing does for them, and a spoken sentence without the
link leaves them to do the work. Scopes: all, and sales talk tracks especially. Status:
adopted. Source: EW-notes-2024-10-05. KB: Script/Language: Linking phrases.

**Three layers of pain, or surface, source, self.** Surface ("I want a fun reception"),
action ("so my guests dance all night"), transformational ("because it's the one night my whole
family is in one room, and I want them to remember it"). Eli's names for the same ladder:
"surface, source, and self, I walk them down it." (27:26) "deepest frame is identity, you're
not selling a new way of thinking, you're selling a different version of themselves." (30:35)
Speak to the deepest layer the listener has shown you, never a layer they have not. Why: the
belief that moves someone lives at the third layer. Scopes: sales calls (ask down through the
layers with "what would that mean to you?"), reels and talks (write to the layer). Status:
adapted; the sources use it to extract pain, this skill uses it to aim the message and to
understand. Source: NLP-3.0-cards (card 52), EW-drills-yt. KB: Framework: Three layers of pain.

**Two sales, and the process comes before the product.** "There's two sales the audience needs
to be sold on." (04:13) "They need to believe the approach, vehicle, process to achieve."
(04:29) Then "you believe your product is the best way for them to take advantage of the
approach and vehicle." (04:40) "it's hard to make the second sale for your product unless
you've made the first." (09:13) Why: the crypto example, a trading software pitched to someone
not yet sold on crypto. For a couple, the first sale is that the reception is run by one voice
and who that voice is decides the night; the DJ is the second. Scopes: sales calls, talks,
reels that carry a CTA. Status: adopted; this is the Trust Trinity's third leg in Eli's own
words. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Framework: Two sales, process before product.

**The through line.** One thesis, said over and over, that every piece of content anchors to.
"The quality of your life is the quality of communication. And you cannot not communicate."
(05:02) "I just keep on saying things like that. That's all ... anchors to my through line."
(05:38) The dance-lessons example: "the key to your great relationship ... is having these
immersive experiences. And the best way to improve this bonding with your wife is through
dance. See how I'm setting it up so all of the content I'm anchoring to that." (06:54) Why:
repetition installs the first sale before the second is mentioned. Scopes: talks, a content
series, a curriculum. Joe's DJ MOJOE through line is already approved copy ("A night your
guests keep talking about, and one you actually get to enjoy"); the WDJM Core 6 beliefs are
the candidates for the WDJM one. Status: adopted. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Framework: The
through line.

**Reframes stay connected, or they are recitation.** The basic shape, verbatim: "It's not this.
It's that. And here's what it means. That is the most basic reframe." (02:45) The warning: a
rep who "said money aside like eight times ... there's no connection. It just, she completely
lost the frame." (02:04) Why: a reframe is a change in the listener's picture, and a recited
technique changes nothing. This is the source for the anti-pattern in SKILL.md. Status:
adopted. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: part of Framework: The reframe.

**A frame is control of focus, and two levers run every belief.** "the frame is like
controlling somebody's focus." (23:27) "whatever you focus on, you feel, two levers run every
belief, focus and meaning. So I'm shifting focus and I'm changing the meaning." (25:16) Eli's
definition of the job: "to be a builder and breaker of belief and a master and molder of
meaning." (24:59) The results chain, for teaching: "results in people's lives come from their
actions, actions come from decisions, decisions come from our thoughts and feelings, and all of
our thoughts and feelings come from our beliefs ... all of your beliefs have come from your
experiences or references." (25:54) Then the honest question: "is it possible that some of the
beliefs that you've created from references in your past could be disempowering you in some
way?" (26:18) Scopes: teaching, talks, mentorship. Guard: the redefine "it's not that you don't
have money, it's just that you don't have the skills" (23:48) plants a deficiency when said to
a couple and is refused there; said to a DJ who asked why bookings are flat it is Joe's own
teaching and is allowed. Status: adopted (mechanism), the money redefine restricted to the
mentorship room. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Framework: A frame is control of focus.

**The story arc: conflict, loss, then delay, deny, distract.** "all stories follow an arc ...
there's a conflict and then there's a sense of loss. And then when people experience a sense of
loss, they usually do one of three things. They delay, they deny, or they get distracted."
(22:10) Why: the listener recognises their own delay, denial or distraction in the story before
the speaker names it. Scopes: Joe's own arc in talks and reels; the before, mistake,
realisation, after of `wdjm-script-writer`'s Story mode maps onto it. Guard: "Most of you have
a victim story, a complaining story" (21:57) said to a room is a verdict; not used on couples,
and in the mentorship room only as an invitation. Status: adopted for structure, the labelling
refused. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Framework: The story arc.

## 4. Make it easy to follow and understand

A belief the listener cannot hold onto is not installed. These lessons are the teaching
layer.

**Teach at the level of principle.** Four levels of teaching: tactics (what to do), strategies
(what and why), principles (the laws that govern the what and why), essence (the energy behind
it). A segment that stays at tactics is a tip. Aim at principle, show one tactic as proof. Why:
a principle travels to situations the speaker never described; a tactic does not. Scopes:
teaching, workshops, mentorship calls, curriculum. Status: adopted. Source:
EW-notes-2024-10-05. KB: Framework: Four levels of teaching.

**Unique, relatable, applicable, and tell them where to go.** Content earns its place when it
is not available elsewhere, the listener can see themselves in it, they can do something with
it today, and it ends with a direction. Why: a lesson that fails any one of the four wastes the
listener's time. Scopes: all teaching and reels. Status: adopted. Source: TW morning session
(as noted by Joe, 2024-10-04). KB: Framework: Unique, relatable, applicable, directed.

**Teach as you give the testimonial.** "Let me give you something you can do. This is what we
did for [someone]. But this is for you." Why: the example carries the proof and the lesson in
one move, and "this is for you" turns a case study into instruction. Scopes: teaching, talks,
sales calls. Guard: the "someone" must be a real, sourced example (`joe-copy-standards` rule 5)
or a bracketed slot. Status: adopted. Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04. KB: Tactic: Teach as you
give the testimonial.

**Make them feel moved before you try to move them.** To move people, feel moved first. What
should the listener feel at the end of this? Decide that before deciding what to say. Why: the
listener catches the speaker's state before the speaker's content. Scopes: all. Status:
adopted. Source: TW morning session, EW-notes-2024-10-04 (know your outcome: feelings, beliefs,
thoughts). KB: Principle: Decide the feeling before the words.

**Reveal the what, the why and the result; hold the how.** Free content installs the belief and
hands over one small win; the full method lives in the paid room. Why: a belief does not need
the how to be installed, and giving the whole how removes the reason to take the next step.
Scopes: reels, community posts, free talks. Status: adopted, and already law in
`process-transcript` and the Knowledge Resources hub; noted here so this skill applies it to
spoken teaching too. Source: Joe's WDJM standards (not an Eli Wilde lesson). KB: not filed,
already in the hub.

**Bridge questions: redirect the focus, out loud.** The question the reader was about to think,
asked before they think it, and pointed at the next thing. "So how do we actually do it?" (10:49)
"Now what do I mean by stagecraft?" (11:02) "So what does that even mean?" (16:44) "Well, let me
ask you this." (19:16) "So the question becomes, how do we actually install a feeling of
certainty and confidence into what they do?" (17:22) "Now here's what you need to realize."
(18:52) Why: a bridge fills the gap the listener would otherwise fall into, and it moves the
focus to relevance ("what does this have to do with you?") or to the next point without a hard
cut. Question control is the rhythm; a bridge is the turn. Scopes: all. In writing, a bridge
is the question-phrased subhead or the one-line paragraph before a section. Status: adopted.
Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Tactic: Bridge questions.

**Tie-downs: get the small yes on the way.** A short agreement check after a point lands.
"Does it make sense? Awesome." (04:51) "Are you with me? This makes sense." (10:49) "See how
there's a flow to that? Yes? Yes." (11:14) "Cool?" (00:56) "Make sense? Yes? Awesome." (24:45)
Why: a listener who has said yes to the last point is carried into the next one, and a room that
answers is a room that is still there. Roughly one every thirty seconds in the session. Scopes:
teaching, talks, sales calls, mentorship. In a reel, at most one, at the turn. In writing, none;
a tie-down on a page reads as needy. Guard: a tie-down asks about understanding ("make sense?"),
never about agreement to buy; the flashcards' "test closing" version is refused. Status:
adopted for the spoken lanes. Source: EW-drills-yt. KB: Tactic: Tie-downs.

## 5. Move to action


**Attention, Trust, Action, in that order.** The close is the last of three jobs, never the
first. Why: an ask that arrives before trust is a pitch; an ask that arrives after it is the
obvious next step. Scopes: all. Status: adopted. Source: EW-notes-2024-07-31,
EW-notes-2024-10-04 (ATA). KB: Framework: Attention, Trust, Action.

**Trust themselves: pre-handle the doubt they already hold, paint the future they want.** The
listener has to believe they can do it and that the result is worth it. The notes record two
tools: an identity shift that answers the objections the listener would otherwise raise later,
and an "irresistible future" the listener can picture. Why: unanswered doubt turns into "I'll
wait". Scopes: talks, sales calls, teaching. Status: adapted, with a hard guard. Answer only
doubts the listener has shown they hold (`joe-copy-standards` rule 9: naming a doubt the
listener did not raise plants it). Never use identity pressure ("people like you sign up") to
force a decision; that half is not adopted (section 6). Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04. KB:
Framework: Trust themselves.

**Criteria questions, rated one to ten, aimed at the yes.** Ask the listener to rate how
important the outcome is, then talk about what makes it a yes. Why: a number the listener said
out loud is a commitment they made to themselves. Scopes: sales calls, workshops. Status:
adapted; used to understand the couple's priorities, never to corner them. Source:
EW-notes-2024-10-04, NLP-3.0-cards (card 152). KB: Tactic: Criteria questions.

**"You already have the perfect strategy for staying where you are."** The notes record this
line: "You already have the perfect strategy, if you want to stay exactly where you are. But
that's not why you're here, is it?" Why: it reframes inaction as a choice with a cost, in the
listener's own terms. Scopes: teaching DJs only. Status: adapted and restricted: usable in a
mentorship room where the DJ asked for help; never said to a couple, where it reads as
pressure. Source: EW-notes-2024-10-04. KB: Script/Language: The perfect strategy line.

**The version-of-you sequence (mentorship room only).** "What do you really want? Are your
current beliefs, decisions, and actions taking you there? The version of you who's already
there, what's different about them?" (31:24) "When would be the most appropriate time for you
to step into that version of yourself?" (32:08) "Which version of you has been in a better
position? The one who feels fear and moves anyway, or the one who keeps delaying because it's
easier?" (34:22) "So what do you need to let go of?" (34:28) Why: "For someone to take a new
action they first have to see themselves as the person who takes it." (30:45) The parts frame
belongs with it: "it sounds like there's a part of you that's a bit scared ... you've got
another part. The part that brought you to this call, the part that's hopeful ... What would
that version of you say to this version of you that's a little scared?" (24:20) Scopes:
mentorship calls and workshops with DJs who asked for help. Status: adapted and restricted.
Refused entirely for couples: choosing a DJ is not stepping into a new identity and the
sequence reads as pressure. In the mentorship room the pressure line "Do you really believe
that? Or are you just trying to be nice?" (32:14) is cut; "why though?" stays. Source:
EW-drills-yt. KB: Framework: The version-of-you sequence.
