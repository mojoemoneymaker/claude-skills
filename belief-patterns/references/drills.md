# Drills

Practice reps, for Joe's own speaking and for teaching DJs inside MC Mastery. Every drill here
comes from the speaker drills video (YouTube `dDCdGJ_TIZ0`) or from Joe's notes of the 2024
events, and says which. A drill is done out loud or on paper, never in the head.

## 1. The favourite colour drill (question control)

From the video (12:49): "Write a sentence like my favorite color is blue. What's a question I
can ask before? What's a question I can ask after?"

1. Write one plain sentence about the thing you teach. ("The grand entrance decides the dance
   floor.")
2. Write the question that goes before it, the one that makes the sentence an answer. ("So
   when does the dance floor actually get decided?")
3. Write the question that goes after it, the one the listener now has. ("Why there?")
4. Answer that one. Then ask the next. Five links long.
5. Read the chain out loud. Anywhere it sounds like a list, the question is missing.

Ten minutes. Do it on a different sentence every day for a week and the rhythm stops being a
technique. Eli's framing: "at first you're kind of staring at the basketball." (01:10)

## 2. Write your vehicle (two sales)

From the video (07:07): "step one for you, write out what is your vehicle or process."

1. One sentence: what must a couple (or a DJ) believe about the PROCESS before your offer makes
   sense? Not about you. About how a great reception (or a booked calendar) happens.
2. One sentence: why your offer is the best way to take advantage of that process.
3. Read them in order. If the second one makes sense without the first, the first is not yet
   a belief, it is a slogan. Rewrite it until the second depends on it.
4. Then list every piece of content from the last month and mark which sentence each one
   served. Content that served neither is content that sold nothing.

## 3. The one-word thesis (thesis pattern)

From the video (14:00, 16:33): "the number one most common thing holding people back from
getting the life they want comes down to one word."

1. Finish the sentence for your audience: "The number one thing holding [working wedding DJs /
   couples planning a reception] back from [the outcome] comes down to one word." Pick the word.
2. Ask "So why is [word] the number one thing?" and answer in two sentences.
3. Ask "So what does that mean for you?" and answer with what they do differently.
4. Say it standing up, once, without the page. If the word changed while you spoke, the first
   word was wrong.

Guard: the "why" is Joe's experience, never a statistic he cannot source. The video contains
the moment Eli reaches for a stat he cannot verify (17:50). The pattern does not need it.

## 4. Reframe your industry's terms (the reframe)

From Joe's notes, 2024-10-04: "Write out 1-2 different common terms in your industry and create
your reframed 'Evolution' of that term." Joe's own worked example was "DJ".

1. Pick a word the listener already uses: "DJ", "playlist", "MC", "packages", "a fun wedding".
2. Write: "Most people are overly focused on ___. What we do is ___, and here's what that
   means for you: ___."
3. Cut the template words. What is left must sound like something Joe would say across a table.
   If it still sounds like the template, it will sound like the template on the mic.
4. End with the confirming question: "Can you see how that's different?"
5. Test on someone who is not a DJ. If they can repeat the reframe back in their own words, it
   landed. If they repeat your words, it was recited.

## 5. Count the modes (vocal variety)

From Joe's notes, 2024-10-04: count numbers aloud in each vocal mode until the three are
distinct. This drill lives in `spoken-script/references/delivery.md`; it is listed here so the
drill set is complete in one place.

## 6. Bridge and tie-down reps

1. Take any ninety-second teaching segment you have already recorded. Transcribe it.
2. Mark every hard cut between points. At each one, write the question the listener had.
   Read the segment again with the questions in.
3. Mark where a point landed. At each one, add a tie-down ("make sense?") and read it again.
   Then remove half of them. The half that survive are the ones where the listener needed to
   say yes before moving on.

## Teaching these to DJs

Drills 1, 3, 4 and 6 are the MC Mastery reps: the grand entrance, the toast intro and the
first-dance handoff are all thesis, bridge and tie-down in a room. Drill 2 belongs to the
Accelerator (positioning and sales). When a drill goes into a lesson, `process-playbook` files
it; this file is the source.
