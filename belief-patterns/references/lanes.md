## Notes per lane

**Reel.** The belief is the first sale. If the CTA points at the free community while the
Accelerator is on waitlist, the second sale is "the room where DJs work on this every week", not
the Accelerator. The thesis pattern gives the hook one word; the reframe gives it a turn; do not
use both in a thirty-second piece.

**Community post.** The two sales map exactly onto Joe's free-versus-premium rule: the post
installs the belief that the process matters, the lesson delivers the process. A post that
teaches the how has made the second sale for free.

**Sales page.** The order of sections is the argument. A page that opens with the offer is the
crypto software pitched to someone not sold on crypto. Open with the reader's situation
described better than they could describe it, then the belief the method rests on, then the
method, then the offer, then the objections the reader actually holds (rule 9), then the ask.
Joe's approved hero line on djmojoe.com ("A night your guests keep talking about, and one you
actually get to enjoy") is the through line for that site; every section under it anchors to it.

**Blog.** The question-phrased H2 is the written bridge. Each section should end on the
sentence that makes the next H2 the obvious question. That is question control at the scale of
a page, and it is also what AI answer engines quote.

**Discovery call.** Joe's system already contains the patterns under his own names. The
Discovery Ladder (clarify the word, find the why, tie to emotion, mirror their exact words) is
surface-source-self. "Get the yes and ask why, sold on you before any pricing" is two sales.
"Comment before each next question so it feels like conversation" is wrap the question.
"Swap the word music for entertainment or experience" is the reframe. The patterns do not
replace his system; they explain why it works and stop a draft from breaking it.

**Objection response.** Joe's own rule from the budget objection entry: "You as the salesperson
have control of what that means. So don't assume that they gave up on you." That is a frame
(control of focus and meaning) in his words. The refused list still applies: no manufactured
doubt, no identity pressure, no "do you really mean that".

**Teaching.** Free room: what, why, result. Paid room: the how, at the level of principle with
one tactic as proof. Both rooms: a question the listener answers before the segment is over.
